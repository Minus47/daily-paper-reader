## 摘要
视觉神经解码旨在从大脑活动中推断感知到的视觉刺激，为人类视觉认知提供重要见解，同时推动脑机接口和人工智能的变革性应用。近年来，预训练视觉-语言模型为该任务提供了语义丰富的表示空间。然而，由于神经响应噪声大、试次间高度可变且具有被试依赖性，EEG/MEG 信号与视觉嵌入之间的可靠对齐仍然具有挑战性。为了解决这一问题，我们提出了一个统一的神经到图像检索框架，通过在共享语义空间中对齐神经和图像表示来提高检索性能。该框架集成了双流自适应、频域校准和相位引导的温度调制，以促进更稳健的跨模态语义对齐。具体而言，双流自适应通过相关性正则化建模低维共享子空间，以抑制冗余噪声并增强跨模态一致性。频域校准使用幅度和相位统计量对神经表征进行条件调制。相位引导的温度调制根据神经响应的稳定性进一步调整对比学习中的监督强度。在 THINGS-EEG 和 THINGS-MEG 上进行的被试内和被试间实验表明，所提框架始终优于强基线。这些结果表明，在异构神经条件下实现稳健的神经到图像检索，得益于稳定的共享语义空间、频域信息驱动的表示校准以及自适应监督。代码将发布于 github.com/wanfkuabc/Geometry-Regularized-Brain-Embeddings。

## Abstract
Visual neural decoding aims to infer perceived visual stimuli from brain activity, offering important insights into human visual cognition while enabling transformative applications in brain computer interfaces and artificial intelligence. Recent advances in pretrained vision-language models have provided a semantically rich representation space for this task. However, reliable alignment between EEG/MEG signals and visual embeddings remains challenging because neural responses are noisy, highly variable across trials, and subject dependent. To address this issue, we propose a unified neural-to-image retrieval framework that improves retrieval performance by aligning neural and image representations in a shared semantic space. The framework integrates dual-stream adaptation, frequency-domain calibration, and phase-guided temperature modulation to promote more robust cross-modal semantic alignment. Specifically, dual-stream adaptation models a low-dimensional shared subspace with correlation regularization to suppress redundant noise and enhance cross-modal consistency. Frequency-domain calibration conditionally modulates neural representations using amplitude and phase statistics. Phase-guided temperature modulation further adjusts the supervision strength in contrastive learning according to the stability of neural responses. Experiments on THINGS-EEG and THINGS-MEG under both intra-subject and inter-subject settings demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms strong baselines. These results suggest that robust neural-to-image retrieval under heterogeneous neural conditions benefits from a stable shared semantic space, frequency-informed representation calibration, and adaptive supervision. Code will be released at github.com/wanfkuabc/Geometry-Regularized-Brain-Embeddings.
