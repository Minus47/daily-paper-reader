Abstract: Electroencephalography (EEG) studies increasingly use paradigms that lack discrete event timing, including resting state, movie watching, story listening, and other nat-uralistic experiments. These recordings contain structured oscillatory activity, but they often lack repeated stimulus onsets that define a shared time clock across trials or participants. We introduce EEGSync, a complex-valued unitary Procrustes temporal alignment method for band-limited analytic EEG signals. EEGSync represents each subject as a matrix of complex oscillatory trajectories and solves a unitary Procrustes problem that maps a target subject into the temporal coordinate system of a reference or group template. The method is compu-tationally tractable through an economy-SVD factorization that avoids constructing the full temporal operator. This temporal alignment strictly preserves the original pairwise relation-ships and power across EEG channels. The unitary transformation goes beyond a simple time shift, aligning latent temporal structure rather than estimating a physical clock offset. Con-sequently, nonlinear instantaneous measures such as phase-locking value, amplitude-envelope correlation, and phase-amplitude coupling are not guaranteed to be invariant. This framing makes EEGSync especially suitable for naturalistic shared-stimulus data, where the stimulus provides a continuous temporal scaffold, and potentially useful for resting-state data when interpreted as covariance-preserving latent manifold alignment. Evaluated under strict subject-independent cross-validation across physiological and clinical EEG settings, EEGSync-based features achieved an AUROC of 0.9600 and a maximum classification accuracy of 89.45% in eyes-open versus eyes-closed state discrimination, using different model configurations. For Alzheimer’s disease versus healthy control classification, the highest AUROC reached 0.9272 in eyes-closed recordings, with additional discrimination observed under eyes-open photostimulation. These findings suggest that temporal alignment captures discriminative information that can complement conventional spectral and statistical EEG features. 1arXiv:2611.24017v1 [eess.SP] 18 Nov 2026 2 1 INTRODUCTION 

# 1 Introduction 

Many EEG and MEG analyses are designed around event timing. In an event-related potential experiment, the same discrete event is repeated many times, and averaging is meaningful because each trial has a known onset. A growing fraction of cognitive and clinical neuroscience, however, studies brain activity during continuous or weakly structured conditions like movie watching, story listening, music, free viewing, and unconstrained rest. These recordings are not stimulus-locked in the ERP sense, yet they are not all equivalent. Naturalistic experiments usually contain a shared external drive, whereas resting-state recordings do not. This distinction is central. During a movie or story, different subjects receive the same continuous sensory input, so it is reasonable to expect a shared but subject-specific response trajectory. During resting state, there is no common stimulus time course. A method that aligns resting-state subjects can still be useful, but its output should be interpreted as an alignment of latent oscillatory state spaces rather than a recovered common waveform. EEG temporal alignment requires care because oscillatory signals have both amplitude and phase, and instantaneous phase is meaningful only after appropriate band-limited preprocessing. These facts mo-tivate a complex-valued formulation based on analytic signals rather than a real-valued alignment of broadband voltages. Conventional alignment strategies present notable limitations, as Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) introduces non-uniform temporal warping that distorts phase-frequency relationships, while spatial hyperalignment or Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) mix electrode channels across subjects, compromising native spatial topology. We propose EEGSync , a unitary Procrustes framework for aligning band-limited analytic EEG across subjects. The method keeps the flexibility of a general temporal unitary matrix, but the interpretation is deliberately modest. EEGSync aligns complex trajectories in a shared latent coordinate system and exactly preserves the pairwise relationships between EEG channels. It does not, without additional assumptions, guarantee preservation of nonlinear instantaneous phase or amplitude-envelope metrics, which are instead evaluated as empirical distortion diagnostics. EEGSync is designed to reduce temporal mismatch between EEG recordings while leaving the electrode channels themselves unchanged. The method therefore aligns temporal structure across recordings with-out mixing information across channels. This work introduces EEGSync, a novel complex unitary temporal alignment framework for aligning an-alytic EEG recordings within a shared latent temporal coordinate system. The method admits an exact Economy-SVD formulation with computational complexity O(V 2T + V 3), avoiding explicit construction of the full T × T temporal operator. EEGSync also preserves the channel power and pairwise inner prod-ucts ZZ † exactly, maintaining the pairwise relationships between EEG channels of each recording while allowing temporal coordinates to be reparameterized. We validate the framework under strict subject-independent evaluation, achieving an AUROC of 0.9600 for eyes-open versus eyes-closed classification and up to 0.9272 for Alzheimer’s disease discrimination. 3

# 2 Related Work 

Cross-subject EEG analysis is challenging because recordings vary substantially across participants in amplitude, phase, spatial distribution, and temporal organization. Existing approaches address this variability through spatial decomposition, covariance and domain alignment, temporal synchronization, or learned shared representations. EEGSync is most closely related to these methods but differs in that it aligns complex EEG recordings exclusively along the temporal dimension while leaving the original electrode coordinates unchanged. 

Spatial Decomposition and Shared EEG Representations. A large body of EEG signal processing focuses on transforming the channel representation into a more informative latent space. Independent Component Analysis (ICA) decomposes multichannel EEG into statistically independent components and has become widely used for source separation and artifact removal (Delorme & Makeig, 2004; Jung et al., 2000; Makeig et al., 1997; Vig´ ario, 1997). Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) similarly constructs spatial filters that maximize differences in band-limited variance between experimental conditions and remains a standard approach for motor-imagery EEG (Pfurtscheller & Lopes da Silva, 1999; Ramoser et al., 2000). Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) constructs linear projections that maximize correlation between signal representations and has been used extensively in EEG, particularly for steady-state evoked responses (Lin et al., 2006). Multiway CCA extends this principle to jointly extract components shared across multiple neural recordings (de Cheveign´ e et al., 2019). More recent neural-network approaches, such as EEGNet and related compact architectures, also learn task-specific spatial and temporal representations directly from EEG (Lawhern et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2020). These approaches can provide highly informative latent representations, but they generally transform, combine, or learn projections of the electrode channels. In contrast, EEGSync retains the original electrode coordinate system and applies its transformation only to temporal coordinates. 

Cross-Subject Covariance and Domain Alignment. A second line of work addresses inter-subject variability by aligning the statistical distributions or covariance structure of EEG recordings. Riemannian methods represent EEG covariance matrices as points on the manifold of symmetric positive-definite matrices and perform classification or transfer in the resulting geometric space (Barachant et al., 2012). This perspective has led to several cross-subject transfer methods. Riemannian transfer approaches align subject-specific covariance distributions before classification (Zanini et al., 2018), while Riemannian Pro-crustes Analysis combines translation, scaling, and rotation in tangent or covariance representations to reduce domain mismatch (Rodrigues et al., 2019). Euclidean Alignment similarly normalizes EEG tri-als using subject-specific covariance information before cross-subject learning (He & Wu, 2020). More broadly, transfer-learning frameworks have been developed to exploit data from previously observed sub-jects (Jayaram et al., 2016), while Manifold Embedded Knowledge Transfer further combines covariance alignment with distribution adaptation in a shared feature space (Zhang & Wu, 2020). These meth-ods demonstrate the importance of reducing inter-subject variability in EEG, but they primarily achieve alignment by modifying covariance or feature representations. EEGSync instead operates directly on the complex multichannel time series and preserves each recording’s channel-to-channel inner-product matrix

Temporal Alignment and Synchronization in EEG. Temporal variability can also be addressed directly along the time axis. Dynamic Time Warping and related approaches locally expand or con-tract temporal trajectories to account for latency differences. In EEG-based brain–computer interfaces, weighted Dynamic Time Warping has been used to improve transfer of motor-imagery recordings across subjects (Giles et al., 2020). Such methods explicitly modify temporal correspondence and can therefore alter local timing, phase, and frequency relationships. This consideration is particularly important for oscillatory EEG, where amplitude and phase dynamics carry physiological information (Cohen, 2014). EEGSync takes a different approach by representing band-limited EEG as a complex analytic signal and applying a unitary transformation to its temporal coordinates rather than explicitly warping physical time. Shared temporal structure has also been studied directly across participants. In naturalistic EEG ex-periments, inter-subject correlation and correlated-component approaches have shown that common continuous stimuli can produce reproducible temporal activity across individuals (Dmochowski et al., 2012). Multi-subject component methods similarly seek common activity patterns across recordings (de Cheveign´ e et al., 2019). These approaches motivate the search for shared structure across participants but typically quantify or extract correlated responses rather than explicitly aligning entire multichannel EEG recordings within a common temporal coordinate system. 

Relation to Representational Alignment in Neuroimaging. Related ideas have been developed outside EEG. Hyperalignment uses Procrustes transformations to construct a common representational space across subjects from fMRI measurements (Haxby et al., 2011). More directly related mathemati-cally, BrainSync aligns resting-state fMRI time series using orthogonal transformations along the temporal dimension while preserving spatial correlation structure (Joshi et al., 2018). These methods provide im-portant conceptual precedents for coordinate alignment in neural data, but they were developed for real-valued, low-frequency fMRI signals rather than oscillatory electrophysiology. 

Positioning of EEGSync. EEGSync combines aspects of these lines of work while addressing a different alignment problem. Unlike ICA, CSP, CCA, and covariance-based transfer methods, it does not construct a new spatial basis or mix electrode channels. Unlike Dynamic Time Warping, it does not impose pointwise correspondence through local expansion or contraction of physical time. Instead, EEGSync represents EEG using complex analytic signals and computes a unitary transformation acting strictly along the temporal dimension. This allows temporal variability to be reduced while exactly preserving channel power and pairwise channel inner products. The resulting framework provides pairwise alignment, population template estimation, and alignment-induced similarity measures within a single formulation tailored to multichannel EEG.

# 8 Experimental Framework and Method Validation 

We evaluate EEGSync from three complementary perspectives. We first verify the numerical properties, including the equivalence of the compact and full Procrustes formulations and preservation of the matrix of pairwise inner products between EEG channels. We then examine alignment behavior through intra subject and inter subject comparisons. Finally, we evaluate whether EEGSync representations preserve physiologically and clinically meaningful information under strict out of sample, subject independent predictive protocols. 

# 8.1 Datasets 

The experimental evaluation uses three EEG datasets that provide complementary validation settings. 

Eyes Open versus Eyes Closed. Eyes open and eyes closed baseline recordings were obtained from the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement and Imagery Database (Goldberger et al., 2000; Schalk et al., 2004). The dataset contains recordings from 109 participants acquired using the BCI2000 system with 64 scalp EEG channels arranged according to the international 10–10 electrode placement system and sampled at 160 Hz. Each participant completed a one minute eyes open baseline recording followed by a one minute eyes closed baseline recording. These paired recordings provide a controlled within subject physiological contrast for evaluating whether EEGSync preserves state dependent neural structure. 

Alzheimer’s Disease, Eyes Closed. The OpenNeuro dataset ds004504 contains routine eyes closed EEG recordings from 88 participants, including 36 individuals with Alzheimer’s disease, 23 with fron-totemporal dementia, and 29 healthy controls (Miltiadous et al., 2023). Recordings were acquired at the 2nd Department of Neurology, AHEPA University Hospital of Thessaloniki, using a Nihon Kohden EEG 2100 clinical EEG system. Nineteen scalp electrodes were positioned according to the international 10–20 system at Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8, T3, C3, Cz, C4, T4, T5, P3, Pz, P4, T6, O1, and O2, with additional mastoid electrodes A1 and A2 used for referencing. Signals were sampled at 500 Hz with a sensitivity of 10 μV/mm , a time constant of 0.3 s, and a high frequency filter at 70 Hz. Electrode impedance was maintained below 5 kΩ , and the distributed recordings used Cz as the common reference. Participants were seated with their eyes closed during acquisition. For the present binary classification analysis, participants with frontotemporal dementia were excluded, leaving the Alzheimer’s disease and healthy control groups. 

Alzheimer’s Disease, Eyes Open with Photostimulation. OpenNeuro dataset ds006036 provides complementary eyes open recordings from the same clinical cohort under intermittent photic stimulation (Ntetska et al., 2025). Recordings were acquired using the same Nihon Kohden EEG 2100 clinical EEG 12 8 EXPERIMENTAL FRAMEWORK AND METHOD VALIDATION

# 8.2 Common Signal Preprocessing 

All recordings were standardized using a common processing pipeline before applying EEGSync. The electrode configuration was retained separately for each dataset, with 64 channels for the PhysioNet eyes-open and eyes-closed recordings and 19 channels for the Alzheimer’s disease recordings. All signals underwent zero-phase bandpass filtering between 0.5 and 45 Hz, notch filtering at 50 and 60 Hz, common-average referencing, resampling to fs = 100 Hz, and channel-wise temporal standardization. Let Xs ∈ RV ×Ts denote the preprocessed recording for subject s. Each channel was standardized according to eXs,v,t = ( Xs,v,t − μs,v )/(σs,v + ϵ), where μs,v and σs,v denote the temporal mean and standard deviation of channel v, and ϵ = 10 −8 prevents numerical division by zero. Within each dataset, recordings were cropped to a common duration T determined by the shortest valid recording and divided into four equal contiguous segments, written as Xs = [ Xs, 1 Xs, 2 Xs, 3 Xs, 4] with 

Xs,j ∈ RV ×(T / 4) . This segmentation provides multiple observations per participant while preserving temporal continuity within each window. Each segment Xs,j was converted to its complex analytic representation Zs,j ∈ CV ×(T / 4) using the Hilbert transform described in Section 3 and normalized to unit Frobenius norm as bZs,j = Zs,j /∥Zs,j ∥F .All subsequent alignment, template estimation, and similarity computations operate on these normalized analytic matrices. 

# 8.3 Subject Independent Evaluation Protocol 

All predictive experiments use subject disjoint cross validation to prevent information leakage across participants. Subjects are partitioned using five fold stratified grouped cross validation with participant identity as the grouping variable, ensuring that all segments belonging to one participant appear entirely within either the training or testing partition of a given fold. Within each fold, class conditional templates are estimated using only training recordings, iterative template refinement does not access test data, feature scalers and normalization parameters are fitted only on the training split, and classifiers are trained without information from held out subjects. # 8.5 Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Testing 

Downstream predictive performance is reported using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, and F1-score. Metrics are computed from pooled out-of-sample predictions across the five test folds. Multiple feature configurations and classifiers were evaluated across the same five subject-independent cross-validation folds, and top configurations were identified by comparing their pooled out-of-fold per-formance. Because model selection was performed directly on these cross-validation results, the reported peak scores should be interpreted as the best observed performance within our evaluation rather than as unbiased generalization estimates for a pre-specified model. For the paired eyes open and eyes closed comparison, statistical significance is evaluated using paired Wilcoxon signed rank tests on subject level EEGSync scores. For the Alzheimer’s disease versus healthy control comparisons, statistical significance is assessed using two-sample Mann–Whitney U tests, while Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests are additionally used to characterize differences between the empirical feature distributions. Numerical invariance measures and alignment errors are reported separately from predictive performance so that mathematical validation remains distinct from physiological and clinical evaluation.

# 9 Discussion 

EEGSync introduces a different perspective on temporal alignment for multichannel EEG. Rather than attempting to match individual time points through local shifts or nonlinear warping, the method aligns recordings through a unitary transformation of their temporal coordinates. Because this transformation acts exclusively along the temporal dimension, the electrode channels are not mixed. This separation between temporal alignment and electrode-space structure is central to the formulation. An important consequence of the unitary constraint is that the channel-to-channel inner-product matrix 

ZZ † is preserved exactly. The singular-value spectrum of the recording is preserved as well. EEGSync can consequently alter how temporal activity is represented while maintaining these global properties of the original multichannel signal. This distinguishes the method from approaches that achieve cross-subject correspondence by transforming or mixing the spatial channel representation. The optimal unitary transformation should be understood as alignment of temporal coordinates rather than as estimation of a simple physical delay. A general unitary transformation can capture correspon-dences that extend beyond time shifts or local temporal deformations. EEGSync therefore provides a latent temporal coordinate system in which two recordings can be compared after reducing differences in their temporal organization that arise from variations across recording sessions and participants. This interpretation is particularly relevant for resting-state EEG, where there is no common external timeline against which participants can be directly synchronized. The similarity and distance induced by the alignment objective provide a natural way to quantify this correspondence. The similarity score ssync measures the maximum agreement attainable between two recordings under unitary temporal alignment, while dsync measures the corresponding minimum residual mismatch. EEGSync uses the value of its own optimization objective to quantify how well two recordings can be brought into correspondence. The group-template formulation extends this idea from pairwise alignment to population analysis. Instead of choosing an arbitrary participant as a reference, recordings are iteratively aligned and averaged to obtain a representative temporal reference for the group. Class-specific templates can then be estimated from training subjects and used to characterize unseen recordings through their relative agreement with each class. The resulting template should not be interpreted as a literal waveform shared by all participants, but rather as a common reference that accounts for differences in temporal organization across recordings and participants. The experimental results support this interpretation of the framework. In the intra-subject and inter-subject comparisons, temporal mismatch decreased after alignment in both settings, while residual mis-match remained greater across subjects than within subjects. EEGSync reduced temporal variability within and across participants while preserving differences between recordings that could not be resolved through optimal alignment. The Eyes-Open versus Eyes-Closed experiment further showed that the aligned representations retained physiologically meaningful information. Under strict subject-independent evaluation, the best EEGSync-based model achieved an AUROC of 0.9600 . The univariate distance and similarity margins were also 19 strongly discriminative, with AUROCs of 0.9589 and 0.9526 , respectively. Clinical validation showed a similar pattern in Alzheimer’s disease classification. For the eyes-closed recordings, combining EEGSync features with spectral features achieved an AUROC of 0.9272 . The spectral-only model obtained slightly higher classification accuracy in this condition, suggesting that EEGSync features provide complementary information rather than uniformly replacing conventional spec-tral features. In the complementary eyes-open photostimulation recordings from the same clinical cohort, EEGSync-based models achieved AUROCs up to 0.8582 . The corresponding distance and similarity mar-gins also showed substantial discrimination between Alzheimer’s disease and healthy controls. These findings demonstrate that EEGSync reduces temporal variability across recordings while preserving information relevant to physiological state and clinical diagnosis. EEGSync features also distinguish between physiological and clinical conditions in previously unseen participants, with all class templates estimated exclusively from training subjects within each cross-validation fold. EEGSync thus provides a framework for temporal alignment and comparison of individual recordings against population references while preserving the original electrode-channel relationships. 

# 10 Limitations 

The flexibility of the unitary transformation introduces an important limitation in interpretation. Ageneral element of U(T ) is substantially broader than a physical time shift or local temporal warp. The resulting transformation should therefore be interpreted as alignment in a latent temporal coordinate system rather than as recovery of a physiological or neural clock offset. This distinction is particularly important in resting-state EEG, where no shared external timing reference exists. The exact preservation guarantee is also limited to quantities encoded by ZZ †. Measures that depend on local temporal structure or nonlinear relationships, including phase-locking value, amplitude-envelope correlation, and phase–amplitude coupling, are not generally invariant under an unrestricted unitary transformation. Changes in these quantities should therefore be treated as diagnostics of alignment-induced distortion rather than as theoretically preserved properties of EEGSync. The present clinical evaluation uses two complementary recording conditions from the same underly-ing clinical cohort rather than independent external cohorts. Validation across additional institutions, acquisition systems, and patient populations will therefore be necessary to establish broader clinical gen-eralizability. Similarly, the current physiological experiments focus primarily on resting-state conditions, while the behavior of EEGSync under naturalistic continuous stimulation remains to be evaluated. Finally, the current formulation places no explicit locality, smoothness, or frequency-specific constraints on the unitary transformation. Future extensions could introduce such structure to provide a continuum between unrestricted latent temporal alignment and more physically interpretable temporal transfor-mations. Naturalistic shared-stimulus paradigms may be particularly useful for studying this trade-off because the common stimulus provides an external temporal reference against which the optimal unitary alignment can be evaluated. 20 11 CONCLUSION 

# 11 Conclusion 

We present EEGSync, a complex unitary temporal alignment framework for multichannel EEG recordings. By applying unitary transformations exclusively along the temporal dimension, EEGSync reduces temporal mismatch without mixing electrode channels and exactly preserves the channel-to-channel inner-product matrix ZZ †. The Economy-SVD formulation reproduces the full temporal Procrustes solution to machine precision while avoiding construction of the T × T temporal operator, making the method practical for long recordings where T ≫ V .Empirical validation confirmed both the mathematical and practical behavior of the framework. The compact and full formulations agreed to approximately machine precision, while the channel inner-product matrix and singular spectrum remained unchanged after alignment. EEGSync also reduced temporal mismatch in both intra-subject and inter-subject comparisons without forcing distinct recordings to become identical. The aligned representations retained physiologically and clinically discriminative information. EEGSync-based features achieved a subject-independent AUROC of 0.9600 for Eyes-Open versus Eyes-Closed classification and up to 0.9272 for Alzheimer’s disease identification in the eyes-closed recordings, with additional discrimination observed under the complementary eyes-open photostimulation condition. EEGSync provides a unified framework for pairwise temporal alignment, population template estimation, and comparison of recordings through alignment-induced similarity and distance. Future extensions incorporating structured temporal constraints and evaluation in naturalistic shared-stimulus settings may further improve the interpretability and applicability of the framework.

# A.7 Numerical and Computational Validation 

We first verified that the economy-SVD implementation reproduces the direct EEGSync solution without approximation. Random complex matrices with V = 19 channels and temporal dimensions ranging from T = 50 to T = 800 were aligned using both the explicit T × T unitary Procrustes solution and the compact formulation of Section 5. Across all tested dimensions and trials, the resulting aligned representations agreed to numerical precision. The median relative Frobenius error was 

3.31 × 10 −15 ,

with a maximum error of 

3.81 × 10 −15 .

These results confirm that the economy-SVD implementation is a numerical reformulation of the same optimization problem rather than a low-rank approximation. We next tested whether EEGSync preserves the pairwise inner products between EEG channels, as predicted by the analysis in Section 4. The channel inner-product matrix was preserved to numerical precision after alignment , with a median relative error of 

3.45 × 10 −15 

and a maximum error of 

5.46 × 10 −15 .

The singular spectrum was similarly unchanged, with a median relative error of 

9.13 × 10 −16 .

This is expected because the squared singular values of Z are the eigenvalues of ZZ †, which is preserved exactly under EEGSync alignment. Finally, we compared the computational scaling of the direct and economy-SVD implementations for A.7 Numerical and Computational Validation 29 

V = 19 channels. At T = 100 , 250 , and 500 , the economy formulation was approximately 3.6×, 13 .1×,and 36 .6× faster, respectively, than explicitly factorizing the temporal cross-product. The economy-SVD implementation continued to scale efficiently to longer recordings, requiring approximately 11 .7 ms at T = 10 ,000 in the present benchmark. In contrast, the direct formulation requires construction and factorization of a T ×T complex matrix, making its computational and memory requirements increasingly unfavorable as recording duration grows. Together, these experiments verify the central implementation claims of EEGSync: the economy-SVD formulation reproduces the full unitary alignment to machine precision, preserves the predicted pairwise relationships between EEG channels, and substantially reduces the computational burden associated with long EEG recordings.
