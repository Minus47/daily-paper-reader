## 摘要
神经追踪，即连续刺激与诱发脑响应之间的时间对齐，使得能够从脑电（EEG）中解码注意，但其在自然视频中的应用仍很大程度上未被探索，且主要依赖线性典型相关分析（CCA）。我们研究一种时间对比模型能否增强EEG-视频对齐，该模型将预训练VideoMAE特征与基于相关性InfoNCE目标训练的轻量级EEG编码器配对。在三种泛化设置下与CCA进行基准比较，该模型在跨被试（留一被试）上优于CCA，在刺激流形内（留一视频片段）与CCA相当，而在外推到全新视频（留一视频）时落后于CCA。由于这一优势仅出现在已见刺激上，我们探究其成因：一项伪控制实验表明，高准确率并不能证明存在真实对齐，因为拟合已见刺激的合成对应关系可以在训练对中不存在真实时间对齐的情况下复现该优势。这并不妨碍实际应用：在已见刺激的下游注意解码任务中，该模型仍优于CCA，因为解码只需要强统计耦合，而不管这种耦合是否具有生理意义。使用组平均EEG参考，我们进一步表明，模型仍然学到了真实且可泛化的耦合，只是它被埋没在单被试EEG的噪声中。这些结果表明，进展取决于数据规模、多样性以及单被试去噪。

## Abstract
Neural tracking, the temporal alignment between a continuous stimulus and the elicited brain response, enables decoding of attention from electroencephalography (EEG), yet its application to naturalistic video remains largely unexplored and mainly relies on linear canonical correlation analysis (CCA). We investigate whether a temporal contrastive model, pairing pretrained VideoMAE features with a lightweight EEG encoder trained on a correlation-based InfoNCE objective, can strengthen EEG-video alignment. Benchmarked against CCA under three generalization regimes, the model outperforms CCA across subjects (leave-one-subject-out), matches it within the stimulus manifold (leave-video-segment-out), and falls behind CCA when extrapolating to entirely novel videos (leave-one-video-out). Because this advantage appears only for seen stimuli, we probe its basis: a sham control shows that a high accuracy does not certify genuine alignment, as a synthesized correspondence fit to the seen stimuli can reproduce it without genuine temporal alignment in the training pairs. This does not preclude practical use: on a downstream attention-decoding task with seen stimuli, the model remains better than CCA since the decoding only requires a strong statistical coupling, regardless of whether that coupling is physiologically meaningful. Using a group-averaged EEG reference, we further show that genuine, generalizable coupling is nonetheless learned, only buried in the noise of single-subject EEG. These results indicate that progress hinges on data scale, diversity, and single-subject denoising.
