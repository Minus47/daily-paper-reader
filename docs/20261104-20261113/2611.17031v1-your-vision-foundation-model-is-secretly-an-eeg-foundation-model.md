## 摘要
脑电图（EEG）基础模型在数千小时的记录上预训练，但其冻结特征向下游任务迁移的效果较差，而且这种迁移并不会随着模型更大或预训练数据更多而持续改善。我们表明，一旦将信号渲染为视觉 Transformer 能够读取的图像，一个从未见过 EEG 的视觉基础模型会比这些专用模型成为更强的冻结 EEG 编码器。我们的方法 SeeEEG 保持视觉骨干冻结，仅学习如何将 EEG 展示给它。每个通道占据一行专用的图像块，并以固定增益的波形或频率范围跨越图像块高度的尺度图绘制，从而使任务相关模式落在单个 token 内。这种显示的蒙太奇、滤波和对比度参数由标准临床蒙太奇初始化，并通过冻结骨干以任务损失进行优化，每个视图最多仅有 1,113 个可训练参数。我们在八个临床、睡眠、情感和脑机接口数据集上，将 SeeEEG 与七个 EEG 基础模型进行比较，所有编码器均冻结并配对相同的读出头。使用冻结的 DINOv3 ViT-B 时，SeeEEG 在所有八个数据集上的平衡准确率均优于每个 EEG 基础模型，并在 Cohen kappa 或 AUROC 上达到 1.3 的平均排名，而最强基线 REVE 为 2.9。学习到的显示无需进一步优化即可迁移到视频编码器 V-JEPA 2.1，后者在八个数据集中的六个上于两项指标均优于每个 EEG 基础模型。将 DINOv3 替换为随机初始化的 ViT-B 会使分数平均降低 15.2 分，因此读取渲染 EEG 的能力来自视觉预训练。

## Abstract
Electroencephalography (EEG) foundation models are pretrained on thousands of hours of recordings, yet their frozen features transfer poorly to downstream tasks, and this transfer does not consistently improve with larger models or more pretraining data. We show that a vision foundation model that has never seen EEG is a stronger frozen EEG encoder than these specialized models, once the signals are rendered as images that a vision transformer can read. Our method, SeeEEG, keeps the vision backbone frozen and learns only how EEG is displayed to it. Each channel occupies a dedicated row of patches and is drawn either as a waveform at a fixed gain or as a scalogram whose frequency range spans the patch height, so that task-relevant patterns fall within individual tokens. The montage, filtering, and contrast parameters of this display are initialized from a standard clinical montage and optimized with the task loss through the frozen backbone, which amounts to at most 1,113 trainable parameters per view. We compare SeeEEG with seven EEG foundation models on eight clinical, sleep, affective, and brain-computer interface datasets, with every encoder frozen and paired with identical readout heads. With a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B, SeeEEG outperforms every EEG foundation model in balanced accuracy on all eight datasets and reaches a mean rank of 1.3 in Cohen kappa or AUROC, compared with 2.9 for the strongest baseline, REVE. The learned displays transfer without further optimization to the video encoder V-JEPA 2.1, which outperforms every EEG foundation model on both metrics on six of the eight datasets. Replacing DINOv3 with a randomly initialized ViT-B lowers the score by 15.2 points on average, so the ability to read rendered EEG comes from visual pretraining.
