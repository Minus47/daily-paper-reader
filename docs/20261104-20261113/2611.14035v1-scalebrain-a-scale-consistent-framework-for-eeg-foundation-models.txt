# ScaleBrain: A Scale-Consistent Framework for EEG Foundation Models 

Minyu Zhou, Yajing Wu, Yongqiang Tang, Wang Jia and Wensheng Zhang 

Abstract —Foundation models pre-trained on large unlabeled electroencephalography (EEG) corpora have emerged as a trans-formative paradigm for Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) ap-plications. Recent EEG foundation models have shown that multi-scale representations derived from fixed-length patches can improve EEG representation learning. Existing frameworks commonly construct temporal scales through feature-level op-erations. However, these operations leave the correspondence between learned features and scale-specific characteristics of the original EEG signals implicit. Moreover, when the self-supervised objective is not aligned with the multi-scale input representations, information at some temporal scales may receive insufficient supervision during pre-training. In light of this, we propose ScaleBrain , a scale-consistent pre-training framework that explicitly coordinates multi-scale representations of EEG from both the model input and optimization objective sides. Specifically, ScaleBrain introduces multi-scale EEG tokenization 

to represent the same EEG segment through analysis windows with different temporal spans and shared temporal anchors, and pairs these representations with scale-specific reconstruction to align with the multi-scale input structure and provide supervision across temporal resolutions during self-supervised learning. We further develop an axial spatio-temporal attention mechanism to contextualize the fused multi-scale representation along spatial and temporal dimensions. Extensive evaluations on six public BCI benchmarks against 12 representative baselines show that ScaleBrain achieves the highest mean Balanced Accuracy on five datasets and ranks third on the remaining one, with only 5.0M parameters, demonstrating strong performance and favorable parameter efficiency. 

Index Terms —Electroencephalography, Foundation models, Multi-scale modeling, Self-supervised learning.

Scalp-based electroencephalography (EEG), which records electrical potentials at the scalp surface, is widely used as a non-invasive brain recording modality due to its high temporal resolution and practical feasibility [3]. By de-coding multi-channel EEG signals, Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) technologies have enabled a wide range of applications, including motor imagery, emotion recognition, sleep staging, and abnormality detection [4]–[7]. Over the past few decades, EEG decoding methods have evolved through three major paradigms: traditional statistical methods [8], [9], task-specific deep learning models [10]– [12], and more recently, EEG foundation models that learn transferable representations across diverse tasks [13]–[15]. This paradigm shift toward EEG foundation models follows the success of large-scale pre-training in natural language processing and computer vision, where Self-Supervised Learn-ing (SSL) enables transferable representation learning from unlabeled data. Similarly, EEG foundation models leverage SSL objectives such as masked signal modeling [15], [16] and neural code prediction [14] to learn from large-scale unlabeled

EEG corpora, capturing transferable patterns in EEG signals without requiring exhaustive manual annotations. Once pre-trained, these representations can be adapted to downstream BCI tasks through fine-tuning, especially when labeled data is limited. However, the effectiveness of such transfer depends on whether the pre-trained representations adequately capture the diverse temporal characteristics of EEG signals. Neural activity can exhibit both slowly varying rhythmic dynamics and rapidly changing transient patterns [17], [18]. These dynamics may be represented at different temporal and spectral resolutions, motivating the use of multiple modeling scales in EEG analysis. As illustrated in Fig. 1(a), recent EEG foundation models such as CSBrain [15] and CodeBrain [19], which build multi-scale representations based on patches and convolutions at the feature level, have demonstrated the po-tential of incorporating multi-scale mechanisms into large-scale EEG representation learning. Nevertheless, existing ap-proaches commonly introduce temporal scales through feature-level operations, potentially overlooking scale-dependent tem-poral and spectral characteristics of the original EEG signals. The correspondence between the resulting representations and these signal characteristics remains implicit. Moreover, when the self-supervised objective is not aligned with the multi-scale representations, information at some temporal scales may receive insufficient supervision during pre-training. 

On the one hand, constructing multiple temporal scales introduces a correspondence issue. Applying an EEG tokenizer on the EEG signals with multiple window lengths produces tokens with different temporal spans and spectral resolutions. Although these tokens originate from the same EEG segment, their temporal semantics are not directly comparable unless their temporal anchors are explicitly aligned. Without such alignment, directly combining heterogeneous tokens may ob-scure the correspondence between representations at different temporal scales and make subsequent multi-scale fusion diffi-cult. On the other hand, multi-scale design at the input side is insufficient if the self-supervised objective does not preserve the same scale structure. Existing EEG foundation models typ-ically rely on a single-scale self-supervised objective, which provides supervision at only one temporal resolution. Such an objective may bias representation learning toward the characteristics of a particular scale and leaves information in other temporal scales insufficiently supervised, leading to potentially incomplete representations. Consequently, an effec-tive multi-scale foundation model should therefore maintain consistency between scale diversity in the input representation and scale-specific supervision in the self-supervised objective. Meanwhile, representations at different scales should be jointly fused and contextualized as they are inherently coupled. In this study, we propose ScaleBrain , a scale-consistent framework for EEG foundation models that explicitly coor-dinates multiple temporal scales during self-supervised pre-training, as illustrated in Fig. 1(b). Specifically, ScaleBrain uses multi-scale tokenization to construct multiple repre-sentations of the same raw EEG segment using analysis windows with different temporal spans but shared temporal anchors, thereby establishing correspondence among tokens at different scales. At each scale, a spectral branch captures time-frequency information while a temporal branch captures waveform-level dynamics. The resulting representations are equipped with scale-aware spatio-temporal position encodings and integrated through gated scale fusion into a unified repre-sentation. The fused representation is then contextualized by an axial spatio-temporal attention mechanism along the spa-tial and temporal dimensions. Finally, ScaleBrain introduces 

scale-specific reconstruction targets so that the scale structure introduced at the input side is explicitly preserved in the self-supervised objective. In this way, ScaleBrain explicitly models scale diversity from both the input and objective sides while maintaining a common temporal reference across scales. The results show that ScaleBrain obtains the highest mean Balanced Accuracy on five datasets and the third-highest on the remaining one, while using only 5.0M parameters, vali-dating the effectiveness of the proposed framework. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Task-Specific EEG Decoding 

Historically, EEG decoding relied heavily on hand-crafted features, such as power spectral density and common spatial patterns, combined with shallow classifiers like support vector machines [8], [9]. With advances in deep learning, Convo-lutional Neural Networks (CNNs) such as EEGNet [10] and DeepConvNet [20] have become standard backbones for cap-turing spatio-temporal patterns. More recently, Transformer-based architectures, such as EEGConformer [21], have been introduced to model long-range temporal dependencies. While these task-specific models have achieved competitive perfor-mance on particular datasets, they are typically trained from scratch, require substantial labeled data, and often suffer from poor generalization due to high inter-subject variability and cross-session domain shifts. 

B. Self-Supervised EEG Foundation Models 

To mitigate the scarcity of labeled EEG data, recent research has shifted toward SSL to build EEG foundation models. JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 3

Pre-trained on massive unlabeled EEG corpora, models like BIOT [13], LaBraM [14], CBraMod [16], BraSTORM [22], and REVE [23] utilize masked autoencoding or contrastive ob-jectives to learn generalizable neural representations. Despite their impressive generalization across diverse downstream tasks, these models typically partition EEG signals into a single series of fixed-length patches. Recent multi-scale foun-dation models have alleviated this limitation by introducing representations at multiple temporal scales. However, how these scales should be temporally coordinated and consistently supervised during pre-training remains less explored. Our proposed ScaleBrain departs from these methodologies by explicitly modeling multi-scale dynamics from both the input and objective sides to form a consistent multi-scale framework. 

C. Multi-Scale EEG Modeling 

Multi-scale modeling has been widely explored in EEG analysis. Traditional signal-processing methods often use band-pass filtering to extract frequency-specific features [24], while task-specific deep models employ temporal convolutions with multiple receptive fields [25], multi-resolution spatio-temporal graph learning [26], or multi-granularity patch-ing [27] to capture patterns at different temporal resolutions. Within EEG foundation modeling, CSBrain [15] and Code-Brain [19] explicitly incorporate multi-scale representations, demonstrating the potential of modeling resolution diversity during pre-training. Nevertheless, the use of multiple scales does not by itself ensure consistent correspondence and super-vision across the pre-training pipeline. In contrast, ScaleBrain treats scale consistency as a property that should be maintained across both the input and objective levels: different analysis windows are temporally aligned through shared anchors, and each scale is associated with a dedicated reconstruction target. This formulation allows scale information and temporal cor-respondence to be preserved from tokenization through self-supervised optimization. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

A. Framework Overview and Notations 

Fig. 2 illustrates the overall framework of ScaleBrain, which reformulates EEG foundation-model pre-training as a scale-consistent masked reconstruction problem, addressing both the need for temporal scale correspondence at the input side and the need for scale-consistent supervision at the objective side. Given an EEG segment, the framework extracts coarse-, medium-, and fine-scale representations using temporally aligned spectral and temporal branches. These scale-specific representations are then masked by a shared span mask, enriched with scale-aware spatio-temporal position encodings, fused into a unified representation, encoded by an axial spatio-temporal Transformer, and finally used to reconstruct masked spectral targets at each scale. Formally, let X ∈ RC×T denote a preprocessed EEG segment, where C0 is a reference electrode set with known co-ordinates following the international 10-10 system, C ⊆ C 0 is the observed electrode set, C = |C| is the number of channels, and T is the number of time points. ScaleBrain defines EEG scales by the temporal span of the analysis window, where sn

denotes the number of sampled timepoints at the n-th scale and corresponds to sn/f s seconds under sampling rate fs.The pre-training is performed over a set of temporal scales 

S = {s1, . . . , s N }. For each scale sn, the model produces a scale-specific representation Esn ∈ RC×M ×D , where M is the number of temporally aligned windows and D is the hidden dimension. The representations from all scales are aligned and fused into E ∈ RC×M ×D , which is then processed by the axial spatio-temporal encoder. 

B. Multi-Scale EEG Tokenization 

To capture the complementary temporal information rep-resented at different scales, we propose a multi-scale EEG tokenization that does not rely on a single series of fixed-length patches but generates temporally aligned tokens across multiple window scales, as presented in Fig. 2(a). Longer windows provide coarser temporal views but finer frequency resolution, making them suitable for characterizing slow rhyth-mic structures, whereas shorter windows provide finer tem-poral localization at the cost of coarser frequency resolution, helping preserve transient and rapidly varying patterns. We use a shared hop size h and define the window anchor locations as τi = ih , where 0 ≤ i ≤ M − 1 denotes the window index, ensuring that tokens from different scales share a common temporal reference. The windows are centred at τi with zero padding at segment boundaries, so M is identical across scales. This explicit temporal correspondence provides a common basis for cross-scale fusion and representation learning.

IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Pre-Training and Downstream Datasets 

Pre-Training Dataset Preprocessing. We pre-train Scale-Brain on the Temple University Hospital EEG corpus (TUEG) [30], which is commonly used in EEG foundation model studies [15], [16]. Following prior preprocessing pro-tocols [14], [16], all signals are filtered by a 0.3-75 Hz band-pass filter and a 60 Hz notch filter, resampled to 200 Hz, and segmented into non-overlapping 30-second EEG samples. This preprocessing yields a total of 1,175,693 samples, correspond-ing to approximately 9,797 hours of EEG data. For consistency with prior foundation-model benchmarks [15], [16], [19], our main comparison follows the commonly used full-TUEG pre-training protocol, in which recordings belonging to TUAB and TUEV datasets are retained in the pre-training corpus. 

Downstream BCI Tasks and Datasets. We conduct evalua-tions across 6 public EEG datasets spanning various represen-tative BCI tasks, as summarized in Table I. These tasks include 

Motor Imagery Classification (PhysioNet-MI [31]), Emotion Recognition (SEED-V [32]), Sleep Staging (HMC [33]), Men-tal Stress Detection (MentalArithmetic [31], [34]), Abnormal-ity Detection (TUAB [30]), and Event Type Classification 

(TUEV [30]). We detail the preprocessing and data split of each dataset in the following.  

> •

PhysioNet-MI [31] is a widely used Motor Imagery (MI) dataset collected from 109 subjects, with four MI tasks: left fist, right fist, both fists, and both feet. The recordings were collected at 160 Hz with 64 electrodes. Following prior work [15], the signals are segmented into 4-second samples and resampled to 200 Hz. We follow the subject-independent split in prior work [15], where subjects 1-70 are used for training, 71-89 for validation, and 90-109 for testing.  

> •

SEED-V [32] is an emotion recognition dataset com-prising 16 subjects, covering five categories of emotion (happy, sad, neutral, disgust, fear). The recordings were collected at 1000 Hz with 62 electrodes. Following prior work [15], we segment the signals into 1-second samples and resample to 200 Hz, and divide the 15 trials of each subject into three equal parts for training, validation, and testing.  

> •

HMC [33] is a large-scale sleep dataset containing 151 subjects, annotated with five standard sleep stages (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM). The recordings were collected at 256 Hz with 4 electrodes. Following prior work [15], the signals are segmented into 30-second samples and resampled to 200 Hz. The data is split in a subject-independent manner, where subjects 1-100 are used for training, 101-125 for validation, and 126-151 for testing.

B. Implementation Details 

Pre-Training Settings. To instantiate the coarse-to-fine tem-poral scales defined in our tokenization module, we use N = 3 

scales, with s1 = 200 , s2 = 100 , and s3 = 40 . The shared hop size is set to h = 20 . We use a masking ratio of r = 50% 

and a span length of l = 10 tokens. The objective weights are fixed as λ1 = 0 .5, λ2 = 1 .0, and λ3 = 2 .0, matching their design purpose. The encoder contains L = 8 Axial ST blocks with hidden dimension D = 192 . We pre-train ScaleBrain for 5 epochs with a batch size of 128. Optimization is performed using AdamW with a learning rate of 2 × 10 −4, weight decay of 1 × 10 −2, and a cosine annealing learning-rate schedule with warmup. The warmup is a linear incremental process from zero to the target learning rate in the 10% of total steps, then the cosine annealing takes over. The detailed pre-training hyperparameters are listed in Table II, which are fixed before evaluation and are not tuned for any individual downstream datasets. Pre-training is conducted on a Linux server equipped with four NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs, each with 24 GB VRAM, using Python 3.12 and PyTorch 2.7.1 with CUDA 12.8.

To fine-tune ScaleBrain on a down-stream dataset, we load the pre-trained weights and replace the reconstruction heads with a classification head composed of temporal or spatial pooling and a two-layer perceptron. D. Evaluation Metrics 

Consistent with prior works [15], [16], we report Balanced Accuracy, AUC-PR, and AUROC for binary classification tasks; Balanced Accuracy, Cohen’s Kappa, and Weighted F1-score for multiclass classification tasks. Balanced Accuracy is the average recall across classes, providing a class-balanced measure of classification performance. Cohen’s Kappa mea-sures agreement between predictions and ground-truth labels beyond chance, while Weighted F1-score averages per-class F1-scores weighted by class support. AUC-PR and AUROC measure the areas under the precision-recall and receiver operating characteristic curves, respectively, with AUC-PR being particularly informative for imbalanced datasets and AUROC assessing the model’s ability to distinguish positive from negative samples. We use Balanced Accuracy as the monitor score to select the best model for evaluation.

G. Further Analyses 

To further analyze ScaleBrain, we study the scale selection, the attention mechanism, and the objective weights. 

Analysis of the Necessity of Scales. To validate the ne-cessity of coarse, medium, and fine scales ( s1 = 200 , s 2 =100 , s 3 = 40 ), we compare the full ScaleBrain model against three single-scale configurations and three double-scale com-binations on four representative datasets. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the multi-scale combinations generally outperform their single-scale counterparts. Furthermore, the three-scale configuration (“Ours”) consistently yields the best or highly competitive results across Balanced Accuracy, Cohen’s Kappa, and Weighted F1. This suggests that coarse-to-fine temporal scales provide complementary information for EEG represen-tation learning. 

Analysis of the Effect of Additional Scales. To investigate whether incorporating more temporal scales beyond the default 

N = 3 can further enhance representation capacity, we conduct experiments with larger scale numbers, specifically 

N = 4 and N = 5 . For N = 4 , we add a new scale s4 = 20 ,and for N = 5 , we further add s5 = 10 . We evaluate these variants across four representative datasets. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the results consistently show that the default N = 3 

configuration yields the most favorable performance among the evaluated configurations. On PhysioNet-MI, SEED-V, and MentalArithmetic, increasing the scale number to N = 4 or 

N = 5 leads to varying degrees of performance degradation and sometimes introduces larger variances. On the TUEV dataset, while the N = 5 variant achieves comparable results to the baseline, it does not provide a meaningful improve-ment. This phenomenon suggests that simply stacking more temporal scales does not necessarily improve representation learning. Instead, an excessive number of scales may introduce redundant representations and increase optimization difficulty. Therefore, the coarse, medium, and fine three-scale partition (N = 3 ) provides a favorable trade-off between temporal cov-erage, representation diversity, and optimization complexity. 

Analysis of the Objective Weights. To validate the effective-ness of the scale-balanced design of the objective weights, i.e. ,

λ1 = 0 .5, λ2 = 1 .0, λ3 = 2 .0 (“Balanced Weights”), we con-duct pre-training with “Equal Weights” ( λ1 = λ2 = λ3 = 1 .0)and evaluate the model on four representative datasets. As illustrated in Fig. 5, the proposed scale-balanced weighting scheme consistently outperforms the equal-weight baseline across all four datasets and all evaluated metrics. Notably, the performance improvements are particularly pronounced on the MentalArithmetic and TUEV datasets. One possible explanation is that the reconstruction targets at different scales exhibit different statistical distributions and energy character-istics. Under equal weighting, these differences may cause the optimization to favor some scales over others. We therefore as-sign progressively larger weights to finer temporal resolutions JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 11 

to reduce optimization bias toward coarse-scale structure and balance the contribution of different temporal scales. These results provide empirical support for our balanced objective weighting design. 

Analysis of the Attention Mechanism. We compare Axial ST attention with the criss-cross attention used in CBraMod [16]. For a fair comparison, the criss-cross variant uses 12 layers to maintain a similar parameter budget. Fig. 6 reports the results on four datasets, where Axial ST achieves competitive or superior performance across the majority of evaluated datasets and metrics, although criss-cross attention performs better on some individual settings. These results suggest that decoupling spatial and temporal attention provides a competitive alterna-tive for contextualizing the fused multi-scale representation, while neither attention design dominates uniformly across all downstream tasks. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose ScaleBrain , a scale-consistent pre-training framework for EEG foundation models that ad-dresses the limitation of current multi-scale pre-training. By incorporating multi-scale input tokenization and scale-specific reconstruction targets, ScaleBrain explicitly models EEG rep-resentations at different temporal resolutions from both the input and optimization objective sides. Scale-aware spatio-temporal position encodings preserve spatial and temporal correspondence across scales, while gated scale fusion and axial spatio-temporal attention integrate and contextualize the resulting multi-scale representations. Extensive evaluations across six BCI benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. ScaleBrain achieves the best mean Balanced Accuracy on five datasets and ranks third on the re-maining one, while using only 5.0M parameters. These results demonstrate that effective multi-scale pre-training requires coordinated representation and supervision across temporal scales. Moving forward, we plan to extend this scale-consistent approach to other neuroimaging modalities such as mag-netoencephalography (MEG), and investigate more efficient attention mechanisms to improve the practicality of large-scale EEG foundation models.
